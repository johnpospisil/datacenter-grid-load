# Pre-publication review (Prompt 19, 2026-11-08)

The report, figures, explorer and code were reviewed as a sceptical grid-industry reader would read them, looking for overclaiming, method holes, EIA-930 quirks and unclear charts. No headline number changed, so the pinned estimates are untouched; all changes are wording, caveats and one chart's source line.

| # | Severity | Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | High | The summary said electric vehicles "don't behave like" a 24/7 load. EV charging leans to the night, so it *does* raise the floor; the point is that it is far too small. | **Fixed.** The bullet names the other around-the-clock loads (crypto, some factories) and says EV charging is too small to explain gigawatts. |
| H2 | High | The Dominion short-baseline caveat said the short baseline made the estimate slightly low. Re-checking the private PJM comparison: growth *since 2022* is practically the same with either baseline (2022 and the latest year shift together). The baseline affects the level relative to 2019, not the headline. | **Fixed** in the report and the explorer's DOM note, in qualitative terms only (decision D3). |
| H3 | High | ERCOT's 3.9 GW of approved large loads was presented as a hard cap ("at most 41%"). It counts only loads that went through ERCOT's large-load process; smaller loads and load served behind the meter aren't in it. | **Fixed.** Now "the large loads ERCOT tracks peak at about 41% of the growth", called a guide, not a cap. Same in README, explorer, robustness chart and utility table. |
| H4 | High | **D3 and a public repo.** The committed docs quoted numbers from the private PJM check, and so did git history. D3 says nothing derived from PJM Data Miner is published or committed. | **Fixed (Prompt 20).** Numbers moved to a git-ignored private file, docs reworded, repo published from a fresh, squashed history. |
| M1 | Medium | The supply bullet said the shortfall "comes over the wires from the rest of PJM" and, in the same sentence, quoted PJM capacity prices, which reads as causal. The body correctly says imports *or* existing plants running harder. | **Fixed.** Both mechanisms named; prices moved to a separate sentence marked as context. |
| M2 | Medium | The 95% intervals (e.g. Dominion 22.9–24.3%) look more precise than the evidence: they cover resampling and baseline-year choice, not model specification (Dominion 17.7–23.9%). | **Fixed.** The table note says what the intervals cover and points to the specification range. |
| M3 | Medium | EIA-930 demand is net of rooftop solar. That lowers measured afternoon demand and growth, especially in California, Arizona and the Northeast, and makes every region look more night-heavy. It was discussed for Phoenix only. | **Fixed.** A general caveat explains it and why the night-time comparison is the robust test. |
| M4 | Medium | The six-region data-center group was chosen after the estimates. | **No change needed.** The report already gives the pre-registered grouping (2–6 points) beside it, and the headline is the 2–10 point range. |
| M5 | Medium | Dominion's "beyond own trend" (+18.3%) rests on a residual trend estimated from 16 months of EIA data. | **No change.** The private PJM check gives a similar 2016–2019 trend, so the figure stands; the main headline doesn't depend on it. |
| M6 | Medium | EIA-930 quirk check: does EIA's DOM zone series drift against PJM's own metered load? It steps up slightly between 2019 and 2020, then stays flat. | **No change.** Flat since 2022, so growth since 2022 is unaffected; it nudges the index level relative to 2019, which the short-baseline caveat already covers qualitatively. |
| L1 | Low | Recent EIA-930 months can be revised, and the last ~3 months of ERA5 are the preliminary ERA5T release. | **Fixed (caveat added).** The stated cut-off stays 2026-10-22. |
| L2 | Low | The robustness chart's source line said large-load data "cap … at about 41%" and cited "(Prompt 2)", which means nothing to a reader. | **Fixed** and redrawn. |
| L3 | Low | Load served behind the meter (e.g. a campus with its own gas plant, as Microsoft plans at Pecos) never appears in grid demand, so this method can't see it. | **Fixed** (named in the ERCOT discussion). |
| L4 | Low | "Neither happened" (the "wrong if" test) was true for the six data-center regions, but Southern, a pre-registered focus region, grew mostly in the daytime. | **Fixed.** Scoped to the six regions, with Southern named as a partial exception. |
| L5 | Low | Data-center load isn't perfectly flat: cooling draws a little more in the afternoon. | **No change.** That makes the floor test, if anything, conservative. |

**Checked and fine:** independent recomputation of Dominion's growth from hourly data (Prompt 15); one row per UTC hour and DST handling; growth as a ratio of sums over the same hours (no average of averages); few hours since 2022 outside the baseline temperature range (≤ 0.15%); chart 8's peak-vs-average and territory differences stated on the chart; COVID shaded on every time series; bar and step charts start at zero; reader-facing chart text uses no internal labels (decision codes, prompt numbers).
